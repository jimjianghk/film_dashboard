Note on data

What counts as a screening?

It isn’t always a film

My definition of “screenings” depends on two things: the type of work shown and how it is sold. The obvious case is a feature film (40 minutes or longer, by the Academy’s definition) on its own ticket. But several other things qualify too.

  1. A feature film screened as part of a double feature (or a triple, or more) on a single ticket. Example: “Moi, un noir” (1958) and “Breathless” (1960), shown at MoMA in November 2025 as a double feature for one admission, count as two screenings.
  2. A short film (under 40 minutes) screened by itself on its own ticket. Example: “Psykho III: the Musical” (1985, 24 minutes) played alone at MoMA in October 2025, and counts as a screening.
  3. A shorts program, ie several short films bundled under a single ticket.
  4. A single episode, or a multi-episode block, of a TV series sold as one admission. Example: “Twin Peaks” (Season 1, 1990) screened at IFC Center in December 2025 in such blocks; each one, such as “Twin Peaks” (Season 1, Episode 1) and “Twin Peaks” (Season 1, Episodes 2 & 3), counts as one screening.
  5. A visit to part of a very long film screened on a loop or as an installation. Example (and the only case so far): “The Clock” (2010), a 24-hour film, played at MoMA in loop, 24/7, from November 2024 to May 2025; each visit, such as “The Clock” (10:31–14:08), counts as a screening.

Apart from the two cases above, shorts do not count. As long as an admission includes at least one feature, any short film shown alongside it does not count as a separate screening or appear in the data at all. The data also exclude any short screened as an installation.
